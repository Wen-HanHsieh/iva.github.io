IVA: Instruct, Verify, Act

Teaching VLA Models to Reject the Impossible

Wen-Han Hsieh *, Elvis Hsieh *, Dantong Niu, Trevor Darrell, Roei Herzig, David M. Chan
UC Berkeley

arXiv PDF Code & Data

Abstract

IVA is a unified framework for Vision-Language-Action models that detects when an instruction is unfulfillable (false premise), clarifies or corrects it in natural language, and then acts safely. Trained with paired true- and false-premise instructions, IVA improves false-premise handling while maintaining strong true-premise task performance.

Why IVA?

  • Most VLAs assume user instructions are always feasible; in practice, humans often give commands with false premises (missing/incorrect objects, impossible constraints).
  • Naïvely following such instructions leads to unsafe or wasted behavior. IVA introduces explicit detection and clarification to keep interactions safe and productive.
  • IVA targets two categories of false premises: In-Domain (plausible but absent) and Out-of-Domain (impossible/absurd) and handles both within a single framework.

Our Solution

  • Single-stage end-to-end tuning: IVA jointly learns false-premise detection, language clarification/correction, and action prediction, rather than decoupled stages.
  • Inputs & Outputs: front camera and the previous 5 joint states → predicted 2-D visual trace + next action as an 8-D vector (7 joint velocities + binary gripper).
  • Architecture: frozen vision & language encoders; an auto-regressive decoder fine-tuned with LoRA adapters to keep training efficient and stable.
  • Instruction template: structured prompt including robot type, control mode, and a short history enables grounded reasoning and action generation.

How did we construct the dataset?

  • Built on RLBench with paired true and false-premise prompts for each episode across 9 tasks.
  • ~800 episodes per task with a mixture: roughly 65% in-domain false premises (injected at ~10% of steps), 20% out-of-domain, remainder true-premise.
  • False-premise prompts cover absence, mismatch (color/shape/count), and impossible actions, encouraging robust detection and safe responses.

Evaluation Protocol

  • Tasks & episodes: 9 RLBench tasks, 25 episodes each (225 total) with randomized object poses and paired prompts.
  • (1) Detection: classify Accept (true-premise) vs Clarify/Refuse (false-premise) and average per-step scores.
  • (2) Execution: when Accept, roll out predicted action sequence; success judged by RLBench success detector.
  • (3) Overall: average detection and execution outcomes for a single overall score.

Results

  • In-Domain FP detection: 100% (perfect) across tasks.
  • Large gains vs. baseline: FP detection accuracy improves substantially; successful responses in false-premise scenarios increase markedly.
  • True-premise performance: maintained at a strong level, comparable to baseline.

Key Takeaways

  • IVA makes VLA systems safer by detecting false premises and asking appropriate clarifying questions or offering valid alternatives.
  • It delivers large gains on false-premise scenarios without sacrificing performance on feasible (true-premise) instructions.
  • The framework is general and can extend to broader robotic tasks, perception inputs, and interaction settings.
